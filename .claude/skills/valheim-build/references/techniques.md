# Design techniques (from research and from what the user approved)

Sources consulted (2026-10): HighGround Gaming's building guide, the Valheim Wiki "house ideas" page,
BisectHosting's best base builds, ExitLag's castle/tavern ideas, Valheimians' Great Hall builds, Steam
community threads. Search again for the specific kind of build at hand: these are the general lessons.

## What separates great builds from bare ones

- **Material contrast with meaning**: a stone base and a lighter upper part; dark wood for braces, corners
  and window surrounds; roof colour different from walls. Pick materials by role (defence vs living).
- **Layered rooflines**: a main roof, smaller overhangs, a second tier over entrances and towers; steep
  roofs for drama. A single flat roof makes a big building look unfinished. Dormers break long roofs.
- **Depth on facades**: plinth at the base, a ledge at each storey line, corbelled cornices, piers that
  rise above the roofline, oriels and bay windows, balconies, overhangs that cast shadow. Offsets of 0.3–
  0.5 m are enough.
- **Window rhythm and hierarchy**: one grid for the whole building; the window type tells the storey
  (barred on the ground floor, tall on the main floor, shorter at the top); arched heads.
- **Visible supports**: posts, beams and knee braces outside (porches) and inside (halls).
- **Height and length variation** between buildings and towers; regular rhythm within each facade.
- **Signature details**: gatehouse with portcullis, bartizans on corners, a slender turret rising above its
  neighbour, crow-stepped gables, chimneys on ridges, a tall tower as focal point.
- **Trim**: frame doors and windows (sills, arch heads), beams along edges.
- **Stairs** turn on landings instead of running straight; wide shallow stairs look finished.
- **Silhouette check**: the outline must read from a distance.

## Interiors

- Timber framing (posts, beams, braces) is the biggest single improvement.
- A focal point (hearth with chimney above, throne on a dais), zones around it (tables and benches, crafting
  corner, sleeping corner), storage near crafting.
- Mixed comfort pieces rather than duplicates: rugs, banners, chairs, tables, beds, braziers, armour stands.
- Lighting chosen where it matters: braziers light large spaces, sconces last longer than torches.
- Trophy walls and shields in great halls; banners can even sit in windows like stained glass.
- Break up oversized rooms with beams, half walls, platforms or furniture groups.

## Landscape

- Shape the ground before building: plinths, terraces, raised ground behind walls, a moat with an earth
  bank, paths cut into slopes, rock formations near towers.
- Light the approach: lamp posts at entrances, along walls and paths.
- Trees and hedges frame views; keep the main facade and the gate visible.

## Lessons from reviews of a castle build

- "Super rough… copy/paste": flat grids of identical blocks and windows. Fix: the depth and rhythm tools
  above, different treatments per building role.
- "Use iron gates for windows… use your imagination": creative reuse of pieces is welcome (iron gates as
  bars and portcullis, crystal as glass, cage walls as leaded lattice, carved dividers as screens).
- "Windows look like doors": stacked shutters. Leaded glass instead.
- "Incoherence": every inconsistency gets noticed (blocked windows, colliding spires, floating poles,
  misoriented parts). Audit and tour before reporting.
- "SUPER bare" interiors: furniture alone is not enough; framing, focal points, light and clutter are.
